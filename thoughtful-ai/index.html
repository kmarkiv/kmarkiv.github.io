01 / Our orientation

The immediate answer is only the beginning.

We evaluate AI by what it leaves people able to do.

A direct answer can be useful. But useful assistance should also make room for people to consider options, navigate their own context, and act with greater understanding and agency the next time.

02 / Design principles

Before, during, and after the interaction.

We work across the full lifecycle of AI assistance: who shapes it, how it responds in context, and what capabilities remain.

Before

Inclusive participation

Whose knowledge, goals, and aspirations shape the system? We begin with local strengths and make absences visible.

During

Context-aware care

How should support change as needs, risks, language, and infrastructure change? We design for care, uncertainty, and human handoffs.

After

Lasting capabilities

What can people still do after AI assistance ends? We look beyond task completion to learning, confidence, agency, and solidarity.

03 / Research in practice

We study AI where its consequences are real.

Our work spans commercial AI, sensitive health and wellness support, and teacher learning in low-infrastructure settings. These are not just test beds: they reveal who current systems serve, where they break down, and how they can strengthen people over time.
